# Activity: Help the Ugly Duckling! 🐣

Create a playful adventure where children help the little duckling complete different challenges throughout the story. The website should feel like a children’s game, not a worksheet or a formal lesson.

## Game sections

### 1. Meet the Animals
Show cute, colorful illustrations of the duckling, mother duck, ducklings, and swans. Children click on each animal to discover its English name and hear or see a simple word presentation.

### 2. Put the Story Together
Show five colorful pictures representing important moments from the story in the wrong order. Children must put them in the correct order by dragging and dropping them or selecting numbered positions. Add a friendly message when they get the order right.

### 3. Play and Learn
Create five simple questions about the story. Use big, colorful answer buttons with pictures where possible. Include multiple-choice and True or False questions. Give encouraging feedback such as “Great job!”, “Almost there! Try again!”, and “You did it!”. Show stars for correct answers and allow children to try again.

### 4. Be a Kind Friend
Show a cartoon scene in which a new little animal is alone because the other animals do not want to play with it. Ask: “What should you do?” Offer three illustrated choices, such as inviting the animal to play, ignoring it, or laughing at it. Give positive, child-friendly feedback that encourages kindness, friendship, and inclusion.

### 5. Make Your Own Happy Ending
Let children choose from cute stickers, animals, and hearts to create a friendship picture. Include a simple sentence they can complete: “A good friend is ______.” Offer words such as “kind,” “happy,” and “helpful.” Display their finished creation with the message “Everyone is special!”

## Visual design

- Use a magical children’s storybook style with cute ducklings, swans, flowers, ponds, clouds, and rainbows.
- Use bright, cheerful colors, large buttons, rounded cards, playful animations, and simple icons.
- Include friendly characters that guide children through the activity.
- Use very simple English instructions, supported by illustrations.
- Keep every screen easy to understand for young children who are just beginning to learn English.
- Add a progress bar, stars, small celebrations, and encouraging messages throughout the game.
- Make the activity responsive and easy to use on computers, tablets, and phones.

## Technical requirements

Integrate the activity directly into the existing repository and follow its current technology and design conventions. All buttons, questions, interactions, scoring, and navigation must work correctly. Include a restart button and a happy completion screen.

The activity must be ready to use with children in a real classroom. Do not create a teacher lesson plan, an academic explanation, or a static mockup. Build the actual playable activity.

**Main message:** “Be kind. Be yourself. Everyone belongs!”
